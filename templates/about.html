Methodology & Limitations

Data sources, assumptions, and honest caveats

Key caveats

  • The exposure portfolio is SYNTHETIC and comprises 600 invented buildings with total insured value KES 6,363,470,000; it is not real client data.
  • The hazard layer is a PROXY using terrain elevation and OpenStreetMap river networks only. It is not measured flood depth and not a calibrated hydrological or hydraulic model.
  • No public Kenya-specific depth-damage curves exist, so the vulnerability function is adapted from JRC/Huizinga curves and parameterised by the backend.
  • Only 12 of 24 government-named hotspots are flagged by the hazard proxy, which is a known limitation and highlights the value of AI drainage signals.
  • Reinsurance treaty structuring, detailed underwriting terms, and policy-specific claims mechanics are out of scope for this prototype.

Data sources

  • OpenStreetMap terrain and river network for the national hazard proxy.
  • Government hotspot names from Nairobi flood-prone area references.
  • AI-extracted drainage findings modelled on public flood-diagnostic material and synthetic summarisation.
  • Public JRC/Huizinga vulnerability curves for adapted damage functions.

Assumptions

  • Return-period mapping: extreme=10, severe=25, moderate=50, occasional=100, common=250 years.
  • Portfolio damage is estimated using the synthetic exposure mix and an adapted vulnerability function.
  • AI-extracted drainage findings enhance the hazard layer, but they should not replace measured hydrological evidence.
